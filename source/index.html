About me.

The longer version — where I've come from, how I teach, and why I only teach two subjects.

I've spent years watching capable students talk themselves into believing they're "just not maths people". Almost every time, the real problem is smaller and older than they think — one missing step from a year or two ago.

That's why I teach the way I do. Before we touch anything new, I find out exactly where the working goes wrong. Then we fix that, slowly and properly, and only then build on top of it. It's less exciting than it sounds — and it works.

I only teach Mathematics and Additional Mathematics. Keeping it to two subjects means I know every topic from NCE to HSC, every common mistake, and every awkward Cambridge question type inside out.

How I teach

Three rules I don't break.

i.

Slow is fast.

Rushing to the next topic with a shaky foundation costs more time later. We go back when we need to.

ii.

Show the working.

Method marks are free marks. Clear, line-by-line working is trained from the very first lesson.

iii.

Boring is good.

By exam day, questions should feel routine. Confidence comes from repetition, not pep talks.

Method

How a lesson runs.

01 / 04

01

Diagnose

A short, unmarked check at the start of every topic. We're not grading — we're looking for the exact line where the working goes wrong.

02

Teach

Whiteboard first, notes second. One idea at a time, with the "why" before the "how" — so the method still makes sense under exam pressure.

03

Drill

Graded question sets, easy to nasty, straight from Cambridge past papers. Mistakes get fixed on the spot, not three weeks later on a test.

04

Review

Every few weeks we loop back with timed past-paper questions. Parents get a short note on what's solid and what still needs work.

Questions

Things parents usually ask.

Should my child take Add Maths in Grade 10?+

If they're comfortable with algebra and thinking about HSC Maths, sciences, engineering, economics or anything quantitative — usually yes. Add Maths makes HSC Maths much easier. If Grade 9 algebra is still shaky, fix that first. You'll get an honest answer after the trial.

Which syllabus do you follow?+

Grade 9 follows the national NCE curriculum. SC and HSC follow the Cambridge O-Level and A-Level syllabuses used in Mauritius, and we work through real Cambridge past papers so the question style is never a surprise.

What happens in the free trial?+

A normal lesson, plus a 15-minute diagnostic at the start. Afterwards you get a short summary of where your child is and what to work on first. No obligation.

English or French?+

Lessons are in English, since that's the language of the exam papers. Explanations in French or Kreol are always fine when they help something click.

Do you set homework?+

Yes, but not much — around 30–45 minutes a week of targeted questions. It's checked at the start of the next lesson, so it actually gets done.

Online or in person?+

Both. Group classes are in person. One-to-one can be either — online sessions use a shared digital whiteboard, so it still feels like working on paper.
